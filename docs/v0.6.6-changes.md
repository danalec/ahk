# The 0.6.6: the tree that is fast, and the enumeration that is live

The record of the release **0.6.6** of the tree. It says what the release
carries, what was measured, and what the tree does not carry yet.

The record of the dialect of the v2.0.32 is in
[`v2.0.32-changes.md`](v2.0.32-changes.md), and the instrument that says what
the tree does not carry is in
[`corpus/v2-corpus-gaps.md`](corpus/v2-corpus-gaps.md).

## What the release carries

The release is the sum of the commits between the 0.6.5 and it, and they are
of three kinds.

**The buffer of the v2 is the memory of the machine.** The bytes of a
`Buffer` were a list of numbers in the property table of the object: a
megabyte of bytes was a million values, and every read of it copied the
whole list. The bytes live in a cell now, and the cell is shared with the
machine: the address of it is the `Ptr` of the buffer, the machine writes
into it without a copy in between, and `NumGet` reads the same bytes. A
thousand reads of a buffer of a megabyte went from **8 062 ms to 0 ms** --
the same answer the official v2.0.32 gives.

**The enumeration of a collection is live.** The enumerator of an array or
of a map was made out of a copy of the pairs of it, and the walk was a walk
of the copy: the entries that the body of the loop added were not seen by
it. The official v2.0.32 sees them -- the `for` over an array of three
items has **four** turns when the body pushes one, and the `for` over a map
of one entry has **two** when the body writes one. The tree gave three and
one. The enumerator walks the collection itself now, and the copy of the
pairs is gone with it.

**The tree is faster.** The work of the release is in the paths that every
script walks: the address of a function is asked to the machine once and
not in every call, the pattern of a regex is compiled once and not in every
call, the tables of the built-ins are looked up with a hash that is fast
for the short names they carry, the encoding of a text and the type of a
number are read without a copy of the name, and the bytes of a number go
out in a block of the stack.

**A script that asks for a version the interpreter does not carry does not
run.** The `#Requires` was checked in the run of the script, so a script that
asked for a `v2.1-alpha` that the tree does not carry ran to the end. The
official v2.0.32 reads the directive with the rest of the script and refuses
the script before it runs: *"This script requires AutoHotkey v2.1-alpha.16.
Current interpreter: AutoHotkey v2.0.32 64-bit"*. The check moved to the
parser, and the module of the requirement moved with it -- the dialect owns
the versions, so the requirement lives in `dialect.rs` and the parser asks
the dialect. The corpus of the upstream: the **488** scripts that the tree
loaded and the official refused are **0**.

**The tree builds again on the machines that are not Windows.** The
`ahk-box` called `GetLastError` and `SetLastError`, which only exist on
Windows, and the build of the release failed on Linux and on macOS. The
two helpers are private now, and on the platforms that have no such call
they do nothing: the last error of the machine is not a thing there.

## What was measured

Every number of this release is a measurement against the binary of the
official release, and every one of them can be run again. The benchmark is
the suite of ten workloads of the v2, scaled ten times so that the clock of
Windows -- which counts in 15,6 ms -- is above the noise, and every number
is the median of three runs of the release build:

| The instrument | What it asks | The answer |
|---|---|---|
| the benchmark of the ten workloads (`target/tmp/bench_suite10a.ahk`, `b.ahk`) | how long does the tree take where the official v2.0.32 takes how long? | **2 485 ms** against **754 ms** |
| the reads of a buffer of a megabyte (`target/tmp/speed_probe.ahk`) | how long does a thousand reads take? | **0 ms**, the same as the official |
| the differential harness (`scripts/differential.py`) | does the real binary and the tree answer the same? | **119 of 119** MATCH |
| the pairs of the upstream (`scripts/pairs-check.py`) | does the same program answer the same in the v1 and in the v2? | **0** gaps of the v1, **0** gaps of the v2 |
| the corpus of the upstream (`scripts/corpus-check.py`) | does the script load, in the tree and in the official v2.0.32? | **0** gaps in 1745 scripts |

The workloads that moved the most, against the official v2.0.32:

| The workload | Before | Now | The official |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `dllcall_200k` | 359 ms | **109 ms** | 16 ms |
| `numput_strget_300k` | 453 ms | **297 ms** | 47 ms |
| `strings_500k` | 578 ms | **438 ms** | 63 ms |
| `regex_match_20k` | 93 ms | **31 ms** | 15 ms |
| `regex_replace_20k` | 94 ms | **63 ms** | 0 ms |
| `arr_for_500k` | 203 ms | **141 ms** | 47 ms |
| `map_for_200k` | 78 ms | **62 ms** | 16 ms |
| `while_2M` | 125 ms | **125 ms** | 188 ms |

The tree is faster than the official on the tight loops of the language, and
it is behind on the calls of the built-ins: the path of a call costs the
tree about 280 ns where the official costs about 42 ns. The handler of the
built-in is not the cost -- it is the path that leads to it: the walk of the
scopes that asks whether the script has a variable of the name of the call,
the checks of the kinds of the arguments, and the machinery of the statement
itself.

## The gaps that are left

There are none of the kind that the tree refuses a script that the official
loads. What is left are the two lists of the other kind, and they are the
rules that need a file or a library that the machine does not have:

- **35** of the corpus name an `#Include` of a library that is not there;
- **2** of the pairs of the v1 and **98** of the pairs of the v2 are
  `#Include` refusals of the same kind.

The table of every one of them, with the numbers and the reasons, is in
[`corpus/v2-corpus-gaps.md`](corpus/v2-corpus-gaps.md).

Three things are left open on the side of the tree, and they are the next
ones to work on:

- the path of a call of a built-in is about seven times the cost of the
  same call in the official, and the walk of the scopes is a fifth of it;
- the properties of an object and the calls of a method are behind the
  official by the same factor;
- the tree does not carry the class `JSON` yet, and the build of the
  headless of the official hangs in `JSON.parse`, so there is no
  measurement of it to compare with.
